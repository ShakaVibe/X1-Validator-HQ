How the cooldown actually works

X1 (and Solana) limit total network stake deactivation to 25% of currently active stake per epoch. If only a small amount is being deactivated network-wide, your stake fully cools in a single epoch — the best case.

When many people deactivate at once, that 25% capacity is split proportionally across everyone. So for an individual account, only a fraction of the cooling stake unlocks each epoch, and the full unstake stretches across multiple epochs.

The slider above lets you model what you're observing: 100% = ideal best case, 50% = roughly what users are reporting on X1 right now, 25% = heavy congestion. Once each portion has cooled, you must call withdraw to actually move the funds — they don't auto-return.

📊 Performance Score Explained

How Scoring Works (v2)

Scores are computed centrally every hour and published for everyone — the score you see is the exact score every other visitor (and your delegators) sees. The formula is size-independent and evaluates pure operator quality over the last 7 epochs plus 7 days of round-the-clock monitoring. Commission and validator age are shown for context but are not scored — commission is a pricing choice, and age isn't something an operator can control. Component scores are continuous: the anchor values listed below are interpolated smoothly, so two validators only share a score when their underlying metrics are genuinely identical.

⚡ Vote Efficiency 30%
Measures how many epoch credits the validator earns compared to the network average over the last 7 completed epochs. This is size-independent since credits are roughly equal for all healthy validators.
Scoring
105%+ = 100 • 101% = 96 • 100% = 93 • 99% = 88 • 97% = 75 • 95% = 65
⏱️ Skip Rate 20%
The percentage of assigned leader slots where no block was produced, aggregated over 7 epochs and confidence-weighted: validators with only a handful of leader slots are blended toward the network average, so a single unlucky skipped slot can no longer destroy a small validator's score.
Scoring (on the confidence-adjusted rate)
~0% = 100 • ≤0.1% = 98 • ≤0.25% = 95 • ≤0.5% = 90 • ≤1% = 85 • ≤2% = 70
📡 Vote Latency 15%
How many slots behind the chain tip the validator's most recent vote lands, sampled around the clock and averaged over 7 days. Fast, well-connected validators vote within 1–2 slots; high latency indicates network or hardware issues even when votes eventually land.
Scoring (7-day average lag)
≤2 slots = 100 • ≤3 = 92 • ≤4 = 85 • ≤5 = 78 • ≤7 = 68 • ≤10 = 55 • ≤15 = 40
🎖️ Uptime 15%
The percentage of hourly checks over the last 7 days where the validator was voting (not delinquent). Monitored continuously by our scoring service — this replaced the old browser-based tracking that caused different viewers to see different scores.
Scoring
100% = 100 • ≥99.9% = 98 • ≥99.5% = 94 • ≥99% = 88 • ≥98% = 78 • ≥95% = 55
🔄 Consistency 10%
How stable the validator's epoch credit earnings are over the last 7 epochs, using Coefficient of Variation. Consistent validators are more predictable and reliable.
Scoring
CV ≤0.5% = 96 • CV ≤1% = 92 • CV ≤2% = 85 • CV ≤3% = 75 • CV ≤5% = 60
🌳 Root Distance 5%
How far the validator's finalized (rooted) slot trails the chain tip, averaged over 7 days. A healthy tower roots ~32 slots behind the tip; a chronically large gap points to replay lag or struggling hardware even when the validator appears active.
Scoring (7-day average distance)
≤45 slots = 100 • ≤60 = 92 • ≤80 = 82 • ≤120 = 70 • ≤200 = 50
💻 Software Version 5%
Whether the validator is running the latest software version in real network use. Staying updated ensures compatibility, security patches, and network improvements.
Scoring
Latest version = 100 • 1 behind = 85 • 2 behind = 60 • 3+ behind = 30 • Major behind = 40
⚠️ Commission-Rug Penalty −15
Commission changes are tracked continuously. A validator that raises commission by 10+ percentage points within a 7-day window takes a flat 15-point penalty and is flagged — protecting delegators from "commission rug" tactics, including raise-then-lower moves timed around epoch boundaries.

Validator Forensics // hardware & fleet analysis

Benchmarks every validator on the work that actually costs money — producing blocks under a deadline — and cross-references operator fleets by co-location, shared performance trajectories, and sibling delinquency. Built to separate real infrastructure from cheap VPS farms collecting delegation.

Idle.
Lag sampler polls every 10s while this view is open. Mean lag ≈ network distance + hardware; variance and max ≈ hardware. Let it run 15+ min before trusting the numbers.

Analyzing on-chain history, then benchmarking block production. This runs a full pass so results appear all at once.

Hardware signal map — low efficiency + high instability = the suspect corner (top-left)

Top suspects — highest farmer risk · click a row to filter the table

Validators

Farmer risk v4 combines evidence with noisy-OR rather than averaging: P = 1 − Π(1 − wᵢ·vᵢ), so independent signals compound and silent ones contribute nothing. (Averaging was the earlier flaw — one damning signal divided by a dozen quiet ones came out a whisper.) Each signal takes the WORSE of (a) an absolute anchor and (b) a robust outlier score — how many noise-widths (median±MAD, floored at measurement resolution) the value sits above the field's own baseline. Identical hardware still scores identically, a uniform field still scores ~0, but validators running 3–10× the field's jitter baseline now register even when the chain is too quiet for absolute thresholds to trip. Weights: shared IP 25% (2 validators on one IP → 70/100, 4+ → max — co-location is the purest farmer signal), efficiency deficit vs the field's p90 20%, epoch instability σ 20%, worst-epoch sag 10%, Slow block production (0.85) and thin blocks (0.50) dominate: the leader benchmark auto-runs with every probe and is the only measurement of the validator doing expensive work under a deadline, taken inside its own 4-slot window so geography cancels — hardware class is the point of the whole exercise, so it carries the most evidence any signal can. Then vote jitter tail 14% (votes missing the next slot — the shared-vCPU fingerprint), arrival jitter 12% (σ of position among each block's votes — sub-slot latency stability that never saturates; distance shifts the mean, only unstable hardware inflates the σ), vote cadence 12% (missing/bunched vote transactions), epoch-boundary stress 12% (late votes during the ~400 slots after rollover — the quiet chain's only guaranteed load spike), slot-1 fumbles 8% + skips 6% (leader-window forensics over ~4.5h — slow boxes disproportionately miss the first slot of their window), clock drift 4%, live stalls/lag 14% (when the live watch has ≥5 min). Trajectory twins (identical multi-epoch damage pattern at 4 decimals, outside the saturated band) score coloc-grade inside the co-location component — independent machines never share exact damage. Fleet mortality 15% (standalone): the delinquency rate of this validator's fleet group (name / style / /16 / trajectory groups of ≥4) — a half-dead fleet indicts its healthy-looking survivors, which is precisely what snapshot metrics can never show. Fleet evidence 15% (matching name pattern after digit-stripping, matching name STYLE template — e.g. one scheme like "X1-" + all-caps word across many different words, identical birth epoch, or ≥3 in one /24 subnet — each capped, since a signal shared by half the chain is demographics, not a fleet; identical self-stake is recorded in the CSV as corroboration but deliberately excluded from scoring) is an amplifier, scaled by the hardware subscore — a fleet of good boxes gains ~nothing, a fleet of weak boxes gets pushed hard. Weights renormalize over available data; hover any risk pill to see which components are driving that score. A ~ marks scores resting on fewer than 3 epochs. A lead for investigation, not proof.
